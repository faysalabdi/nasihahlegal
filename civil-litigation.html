Practice area I

Civil litigation

Disputes about money, property, contracts and conduct — resolved by negotiation where we can, and by court or tribunal proceedings where we must.

Have a hearing date or a letter of demand? Time limits matter. Call us before you reply.

0426 988 250

Most civil disputes are won or lost long before a hearing — in the quality of the documents, the strength of the evidence and the judgement calls made early. We give you a frank assessment of your position, then run the matter in a way that keeps the cost proportionate to what is at stake.

We act for both plaintiffs and defendants: people chasing money they are owed, people defending claims they say are wrong, businesses in dispute with suppliers or customers, and homeowners in dispute with builders. Our first advice is always about whether the fight is worth having.

How we can help

Contract disputes

Agreements that have broken down — unpaid work, services not delivered, terms in dispute, or a party walking away. We advise on what the contract actually obliges each side to do, what a breach is worth, and whether to enforce, renegotiate or terminate.

Employment and workplace disputes

Disputes arising out of the working relationship, including contract and entitlement disputes, post-employment restraints and workplace conduct issues. We advise on your position early, when the options are widest, and on the forum best suited to resolving it.

Magistrates’, County and Supreme Court disputes

We act in civil proceedings at every level of the Victorian court system, from smaller claims in the Magistrates’ Court through to larger or more complex matters in the County and Supreme Courts. We advise on which court suits your claim, prepare the pleadings and evidence, and appear at mediations and hearings.

Motor vehicle accidents

Property damage claims after a collision — recovering repair costs and losses from the other driver or their insurer, or defending a claim made against you. We deal with insurers, assessors and recovery agents, and advise on whether a claim should be settled or fought.

Building and construction disputes

Defective or incomplete work, variations, delays, progress claims and termination. We advise homeowners and builders on rights under the building contract and Victorian legislation, and act in domestic building disputes, including matters that must first go to Domestic Building Dispute Resolution Victoria before a VCAT proceeding.

Debt recovery

Letters of demand, court proceedings and enforcement for unpaid invoices and loans — and defence work where a debt is disputed, has been paid, or is statute-barred. For businesses, we can also review your terms of trade so the next debt is easier to recover.

Consumer law matters

Faulty goods, services that were never delivered, misleading conduct and unfair contract terms under the Australian Consumer Law. We advise on the remedies available to you and pursue them in the appropriate forum.

VCAT matters

Representation in the Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal, including domestic building, goods and services, owners corporation and retail tenancy disputes. VCAT is designed to be accessible, but preparation still decides outcomes — and in some VCAT lists you need permission to be legally represented, which we can apply for.

What to expect

  1. Assessment. We read your documents and tell you what your claim or defence is really worth, what it will cost to pursue, and what the risks are — including the risk of paying the other side’s costs.
  2. Pre-litigation steps. A properly drafted letter resolves a surprising number of disputes. Where a pre-action process applies, we comply with it and use it to narrow the issues.
  3. Proceedings. If the matter doesn’t resolve, we issue or defend, prepare witness statements and discovery, and keep the pleadings tight.
  4. Mediation and hearing. Most civil matters settle at mediation. We prepare for it as seriously as for a hearing — and if it doesn’t settle, we’re ready for the hearing that follows.
Time limits apply. In Victoria, many civil claims must be started within six years, and some — including personal injury and certain statutory claims — much sooner. If you think you may have a claim, get advice early rather than later.

Common questions

How much does a civil case cost?

It depends on the value and complexity of the dispute, and on how the other side behaves. Before we start, you receive a costs disclosure with an estimate for each stage, and we update it if the matter changes course. For discrete pieces of work — a letter of demand, a single appearance — we can usually quote a fixed fee.

If I win, does the other side pay my legal costs?

Usually a successful party recovers some of its costs, but rarely all of them. Recovery is also only as good as the other party’s ability to pay. We factor both into the advice we give you at the start — the question is never only “can I win?” but “what will I actually be left with?”.

Should I go to mediation?

In most cases, yes — and in many proceedings it is compulsory. Mediation lets you control the outcome instead of handing it to a judge, and settlements can include terms a court could never order, such as payment plans, apologies or future work. We prepare you for the day so you can negotiate from a position of knowledge.

Can I run my case myself?

You can, and in smaller matters it is sometimes sensible. We’re happy to advise on a limited basis — reviewing your documents or preparing you for a hearing you will run yourself — if full representation isn’t proportionate to what’s at stake.
