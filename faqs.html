Working with us

How do I make an appointment?

Call 0426 988 250, email info@nasihahlegal.com.au, or send the enquiry form. Tell us briefly what the matter is about and include any court or tribunal dates so we can prioritise urgent matters.

What should I bring to the first appointment?

Bring everything you have, even if it seems minor: letters, contracts, court documents, invoices, photographs, screenshots of texts and emails, and identification. A short written timeline of what happened and when is the single most useful thing you can prepare.

Can we meet by phone or video instead?

Yes. Appointments can be in person at our Coburg office, by telephone or by video, whichever suits you. Documents can be sent and signed electronically in most matters.

Is what I tell you confidential?

Yes. What you tell us in the course of getting legal advice is confidential and generally protected by legal professional privilege. That protection applies from the first conversation, whether or not you go on to engage us. Please still avoid sending sensitive details in your very first email or form message.

Fees & costs

How are your fees charged?

By fixed fee where the work can be scoped in advance — conveyancing, a simple will, a single court appearance — and otherwise at an hourly rate with written estimates for each stage. Either way, you receive a costs disclosure before the work starts.

Will I know if costs are going to increase?

Yes. If a matter changes direction and our estimate needs to change, we tell you before the additional work is done, not after.

Does it cost anything to make an enquiry?

No. Asking whether we can help costs nothing, and we will tell you honestly if your matter is not one we take on or not worth pursuing. If an appointment is the right next step, you will know what it costs before you book it.

Can you give me a fixed fee?

For work that can be scoped in advance, yes — conveyancing, a straightforward will, a letter of demand or a single court appearance. Where the shape of a matter depends on what the other side does, we charge at an hourly rate and give you a written estimate for each stage instead.

Civil disputes

Someone owes me money. What can I do?

Start with a properly drafted letter of demand — it resolves many disputes on its own. If it doesn’t, proceedings can be issued in the appropriate court, and a judgment can then be enforced. We advise first on whether the other party can actually pay, because a judgment against an empty pocket is an expensive piece of paper.

How long do I have to start a claim?

Many civil claims in Victoria must be commenced within six years, but shorter limits apply to some claims, including personal injury and certain claims against public bodies. Don’t assume you have time — get advice as soon as the dispute arises.

My builder has done defective work. Where does that go?

Most domestic building disputes must first go through Domestic Building Dispute Resolution Victoria before an application can be made to VCAT. Keep every variation, invoice, photograph and message — building disputes are won on records.

Do I need a lawyer at VCAT?

Not always, and in some lists permission is required before a party can be legally represented. That said, preparation still decides most outcomes, and we can help behind the scenes even where we don’t appear.

Criminal & traffic

Police want to interview me. Should I go?

Get advice first. You generally must give your name and address, but you are not obliged to answer other questions, and what you say can be used in evidence. Call us before the interview, not after.

Will I lose my licence?

For some offences a period of licence loss is mandatory and a court has no discretion; for others there is room to argue. We tell you which category your charge falls into at the first conference, along with anything that may reduce the period.

What is diversion, and can I get it?

Diversion allows an eligible matter to be resolved without a finding of guilt, usually on conditions such as a donation, a letter of apology or a course. It requires the informant’s consent and an acknowledgement of responsibility. We assess eligibility and prepare the application material.

Do I have to attend court myself?

In many matters, yes — particularly pleas and contested hearings. In some administrative listings we can appear on your behalf. We’ll tell you exactly which dates you must attend.

Family law

Do I need to be divorced before dividing property?

No. Property can be divided after separation, before any divorce. But once a divorce becomes final, a 12-month deadline applies to property applications — and for de facto relationships the limit is two years from separation.

How are children’s arrangements decided?

By what is in the best interests of the child, with safety the paramount consideration. Most parents reach agreement through family dispute resolution and record it in a parenting plan or consent orders; the court decides only where agreement isn’t possible.

Is a Binding Financial Agreement worth doing?

It can be, particularly where one party brings significant assets into a relationship, or where separating couples want certainty without court. The formal requirements are strict — including independent legal advice for both parties — and a poorly drafted agreement can be set aside.

What if my ex won’t disclose their finances?

Full and frank disclosure is a duty, not an option. Where it is ignored, orders can be sought, subpoenas issued, and adverse inferences drawn against the party who withheld information.

Wills & estates

How long do I have to contest a will?

In Victoria, a family provision application generally must be made within six months of the grant of probate or letters of administration. Late applications need the court’s permission and are not always allowed.

I’m an executor. What do I actually have to do?

Locate the will, identify and protect the assets, apply for probate where required, pay debts and taxes, keep proper accounts, and distribute to the beneficiaries. You must act in the interests of the estate as a whole — and you can be held personally liable if you get it wrong.

How often should I update my will?

After any significant change: marriage, separation or divorce, a new child or grandchild, a death in the family, buying or selling property, or starting a business. Marriage and divorce can affect an existing will, so review it whenever your circumstances shift.

What happens if there is no will?

The estate is distributed according to the intestacy rules, which set out who takes and in what shares. That order may bear little resemblance to what the person would have wanted, and it often makes administration slower and more expensive.

Property

Should I have the contract reviewed before I sign?

Always. Once you sign — or bid successfully at auction — your options narrow sharply. Send us the contract and section 32 and we will go through it with you before you are committed.

What is a section 32?

It is the vendor statement: the disclosure document a seller must give a buyer before signing, covering title, rates and outgoings, planning and building information, easements and other matters. Defects in it can give a purchaser rights, which is exactly why it should be read carefully.

How long does settlement take?

Whatever period the contract sets — commonly 30, 60 or 90 days. Settlement itself is electronic, and we confirm with you as soon as it has completed.

My neighbour’s fence is on my land. What now?

Start with a survey so everyone is working from facts rather than assumptions. From there the options range from an agreed adjustment to a fencing notice or, in long-standing cases, an adverse possession application by whichever party has occupied the land.
