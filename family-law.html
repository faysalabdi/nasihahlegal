Practice area III

Family law

Separation is hard enough without legal uncertainty. We help you sort out arrangements for the children and a fair division of property — calmly, and without unnecessary court.

Worried about safety? If you or your children are in danger, call 000. We can help with protection order applications afterwards.

Make an enquiry

Family law outcomes are rarely about winning. They are about workable arrangements that let both households function and let children keep a relationship with both parents where that is safe. Our job is to get you there with as little damage as possible.

We act for people at every stage: those considering separation, those in the middle of negotiations, and those who need to respond to a court application. Where agreement is possible, we document it properly so it is binding. Where it isn’t, we are ready to litigate.

How we can help

Divorce and separation

The practical first steps after a separation: what to do about the house, the bank accounts and the children in the short term, how separation is proved, and when a divorce application can be made. A divorce ends the marriage — it does not divide property or settle parenting, and the deadlines that follow it matter.

Property and financial matters

Identifying and valuing the asset pool, including superannuation, businesses and debts; advising on contributions and future needs; and negotiating a settlement. Agreements can be formalised by consent orders so the division is final and, in most cases, free of stamp duty on transfers between separating partners.

Children’s matters

Where children live, how time is shared, schooling, travel and relocation, and how decisions are made. The law asks what is in the best interests of the child — we help you frame proposals in those terms, through parenting plans or consent orders, and represent you if court becomes necessary.

Binding Financial Agreements

Agreements made before, during or after a relationship that set out how property will be divided. They have strict formal requirements — including independent legal advice for each party — and we prepare and advise on them so they hold up when they are needed.

Consent orders

Where you have reached agreement, consent orders make it binding and enforceable without a contested hearing. We draft the orders and the supporting application so the court can be satisfied the arrangement is just and equitable, or in the children’s best interests.

Dispute resolution and mediation

Family dispute resolution is required before most parenting applications, and mediation resolves many property matters. With a solicitor and mediator in the firm, we can prepare you for mediation, attend with you, and turn any agreement reached into an enforceable document.

What to expect

  1. First appointment. We take your history, explain how property and parenting are approached, and identify anything urgent.
  2. Disclosure. Both parties must disclose their financial position honestly. We help you gather documents and press for theirs.
  3. Negotiation or mediation. Proposals are exchanged, with the aim of settling on terms you can live with.
  4. Formalising or filing. Agreements become consent orders or a Binding Financial Agreement; if there is no agreement, we prepare the court application.
Deadlines to know. A property application must generally be made within 12 months of a divorce becoming final, or within two years of the end of a de facto relationship. Later applications need the court’s permission, which is not guaranteed.

Common questions

Is everything split 50/50?

No. There is no fixed formula. A court considers the assets and debts of both parties, the financial and non-financial contributions each made (including as homemaker and parent), and each person’s future needs — such as care of children, health and earning capacity. Outcomes vary widely.

Do we have to go to court?

Most matters never reach a final hearing. Parenting matters generally require family dispute resolution first, and property matters are commonly settled at mediation. Court remains available where there is risk, urgency, non-disclosure, or simply no prospect of agreement.

Can we use the same lawyer to save money?

No — a lawyer can only act for one party. For Binding Financial Agreements, independent advice for each person is a legal requirement, not just good practice.

What about family violence?

Tell us early. Family violence affects parenting arrangements, property outcomes and the way negotiations are conducted. We can also advise on intervention orders and arrange safe ways to communicate with the other party.
