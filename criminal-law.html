Practice area II

Criminal law

Charged, summonsed, or asked to attend an interview? Get advice before you say anything. What happens in the first week often shapes the result.

Court date coming up? Call us — even if it’s close.

0426 988 250

A criminal charge affects far more than the day in court — your licence, your employment, your visa, your family. We explain exactly what you are facing, what the prosecution has to prove, and the options that are genuinely open to you.

We act in the Magistrates’ Court and in appeals and committal work beyond it. Whether the sensible path is a well-prepared plea, a contest, or an application to have the matter dealt with by diversion, we prepare properly — the material put before a magistrate matters as much as the submissions made on the day.

How we can help

Assault charges and family violence matters

Assault and related charges, and matters involving intervention orders — including applications made against you and breaches alleged after an order is in place. These matters move quickly and affect where you can live and when you can see your children, so early advice matters.

Drug offences

Possession, use, trafficking and cultivation charges. We examine how the evidence was obtained — searches, warrants and admissions — before advising on plea or contest, and prepare the material that supports treatment-focused outcomes where they are available.

Theft and dishonesty offences

Theft, handling, obtaining property or financial advantage by deception, and related charges. Restitution, timing and a properly prepared plea often make the difference between a conviction and a non-conviction outcome.

Sexual offences

Serious charges that require careful, methodical defence work from the first day — obtaining and analysing the brief, subpoenaing material, advising on committal, and briefing experienced counsel where the matter proceeds to a higher court.

Firearms and weapons offences

Charges relating to prohibited weapons, unregistered or unsecured firearms, and licence conditions, including the consequences for your firearms licence and for employment that depends on it.

Traffic offences and licence matters

Drink and drug driving, careless and dangerous driving, excessive speed, unlicensed driving, and applications relating to licence loss. We advise on mandatory minimum periods, interlock conditions and what a court can and cannot do about your licence.

Pleas, contest mentions and diversion

Whatever the charge, the path through the Magistrates’ Court is the same: a well-prepared plea with real mitigation material, a contest mention used to narrow the issues and negotiate with the informant, or a diversion application where you are eligible and the charge can be resolved without a finding of guilt.

Advice before a police interview

If police have asked you to attend an interview, speak with us first. We explain your right to silence, what you are obliged to do and what you are not, and how what you say may be used later.

What to expect

  1. First conference. Bring your charge sheet, bail papers and anything police have given you. We explain the charges and the likely path of the matter.
  2. The brief. We obtain and review the prosecution brief — statements, records of interview, CCTV, certificates — and advise on the strength of the case against you.
  3. Negotiation. Where appropriate, we negotiate with the informant about charges, the summary of facts or diversion.
  4. Court. We appear with you, having prepared your material in advance, and explain the result and what it means before you leave.
If you have been arrested or are at the police station now, ask to speak to a lawyer and call us on 0426 988 250. You do not have to answer questions beyond confirming your name and address.

Common questions

Will I get a criminal record?

Not every finding of guilt results in a recorded conviction. A court can, in appropriate cases, find the charge proven without recording a conviction, and diversion avoids a finding of guilt altogether. What is realistic in your case depends on the offence, your history and the material we can put before the court.

Do I have to plead guilty because I made admissions to police?

No. Admissions are one part of a brief and are not always admissible or accurate. We review how the interview was conducted and what the rest of the evidence shows before advising you on how to plead.

What is a contest mention, exactly?

It is a hearing before a magistrate where the parties identify the real issues in dispute and explore resolution. Many matters finish there — with charges withdrawn or amended, or with a plea on a revised summary — without the time and cost of a contested hearing.

Can you help with a matter outside Melbourne?

Often, yes. Tell us which court your matter is listed in and we’ll tell you honestly whether we are the right firm to act, and if not, who is.
